What the audit covers

Six areas we check

We review everything you tell customers about the environment: on packaging, in your online store, on marketplaces and in ads.

Generic claims

“Eco”, “green”, “environmentally friendly” and similar. Without proven, recognised excellent environmental performance they are banned.

Climate neutrality

“Climate neutral” claims based on offsetting emissions are no longer allowed.

Labels and logos

Own sustainability labels must be based on a certification scheme or set up by a public authority.

Whole product or part

A claim about the whole product cannot rely on one element only, such as the packaging.

Future targets

Promises such as “net zero by 2030” need an implementation plan and independent verification.

Evidence

Documentation backing every claim, ready for a customer, a marketplace or a regulator.

FAQ

Greenwashing questions

What is greenwashing?

Misleading consumers about the environmental impact of a product or company, for example with vague claims without proof or confusing labels.

What changed on 27 September 2026?

National rules transposing Directive (EU) 2024/825 started to apply. Generic environmental claims without proof, offset-based climate neutrality claims and uncertified sustainability labels are now unfair commercial practices.

Can I still say my product is eco-friendly?

Yes, if you state the specific benefit and can prove it, for example “packaging made from 100% recycled paper”. A bare “eco” without justification is risky.

What are the penalties?

Penalties are set nationally. In Poland, the consumer authority (UOKiK) can fine up to 10% of turnover for practices harming collective consumer interests. There is also the risk of consumer claims and blocked marketplace listings.

How long does an audit take?

It depends on the number of products and sales channels. We give you a timeline and a quote after a free consultation.
